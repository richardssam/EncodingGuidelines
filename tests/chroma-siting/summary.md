# Chroma siting when converting RGB to 4:2:2 and 4:2:0

Each converter converts `sourceimages/chroma_siting_chart.png` (16-bit RGB, untagged) to 16-bit 4:2:2 or 4:2:0. Positions are in luma samples: **co-sited** (0) means the chroma sample sits on the even luma sample, as professional 4:2:2 and H.264/HEVC 4:2:0 expect (`left`); **centre** (0.5) means half way between two luma samples (JPEG/MPEG-1 style). H.264/HEVC 4:2:0 (`left`) is co-sited horizontally and centred vertically. Each cell has these lines:

| Line | Example | What it is | Correct for pro 4:2:2 / H.264 4:2:0 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Position | `co-sited` / `centre` | Measured from the chart's tents: where the chroma samples actually are. For 4:2:0, horizontal / vertical. | `co-sited` (4:2:0: `co-sited / centre`) |
| Lines | `consistent (0 / 0)` | The position measured from single-pixel lines on even and on odd columns (rows). A correct filter gives the same for both. **pair-averaged** means they differ by about 1: the converter averaged pairs of pixels before filtering, which loses the real siting (libswscale does this for RGB input unless `full_chroma_inp` is set). | `consistent` |
| Tag | `tag not written` | The chroma location written to the H.264 stream by a lossless encode. `not written` means readers use the H.264 default, `left` (ffprobe also shows `left`). H.264 only signals this for 4:2:0; 4:2:2 has no field and is assumed co-sited. ProRes and DNxHD have no field at all. | `left` or `not written` |
